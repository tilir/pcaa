Across all 11 entries (1,878 nodes), 433,899 original MMIO submissions become
1,860 implemented software-built batches: 432,039 fewer submissions (99.6%).
All 433,899 child primitive descriptors remain. Outer batch descriptors occupy
104,160 bytes, child descriptors 24,298,344 bytes, logical operand reads
107,235,704 bytes, and argmin results 3,471,192 bytes.

Per-primitive packing totals 40,939,596 bytes. Reusing identical strided views
within a batch reduces that to 2,834,520 bytes (93.1%). The largest synthetic
batch has 4,096 primitive descriptors; host-only stress profiles may exceed the
fixed bare-metal PBQP domain limits.

The report distinguishes three models:

1. Original primitive submission: one doorbell and descriptor per primitive,
   with per-primitive packing.
2. Implemented software-built batch: one doorbell per R1/R2, child primitive
   descriptors retained, and repeated strided views packed once.
3. Hypothetical structural batch: one higher-level descriptor per R1/R2; it is
   counted analytically only and is not implemented.

Software batching removes control traffic and most packing traffic, while
primitive descriptor traffic remains material. Stride-aware operands and
structural descriptors therefore remain open design questions.
